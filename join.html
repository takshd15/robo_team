RoboTeam Twente

RoboTeam Twente is a student team from the University of Twente and Saxion University of Applied Sciences. Students from different engineering programmes design, build and program robots together, and get hands-on experience outside the regular curriculum.

Right now we’re building a Mars rover for the European Rover Challenge. University teams from around the world bring their rovers to a simulated Martian site, where each rover has to navigate on its own, cross rough terrain and analyse its surroundings. Students in mechanical engineering, electronics, embedded systems, control engineering and AI design the rover together, integrate it and drive it.

The team is a place to try things and get real engineering experience. Members learn to design and integrate complex robotic systems, work with people from other engineering disciplines, run a large technical project and solve problems that come from outside the classroom.

Before Mars we played robot football. For years the team competed in RoboCup with autonomous soccer robots and travelled to competitions around the world as the robots improved. The same team now builds robots for space exploration.

Ten crews, one team

Nine RoboCup seasons with football robots, then the switch to a Mars rover. Each crew is one academic year.

  1. 2025–26Crew 10

    Moved the team from football robots to a Mars rover and ranked 59th of 124 teams in the European Rover Challenge 2026.

  2. 2024–25Crew 9

    Made big progress on reinforcement learning for the robots and organised a one-off competition of its own in Hannover.

  3. 2023–24Crew 8

    Third in the Passing Challenge and sixth overall at RoboCup 2024 in Eindhoven. The Passing Challenge is one of the hardest technical tests in robot football.

  4. 2022–23Crew 7

    The first crew to win RoboCup’s open-source award. It also carried on rolling out the team’s new visual identity.

  5. 2021–22Crew 6

    Third at RoboCup in Bangkok, and the first crew under the purple banner and logo.

  6. 2020–21Crew 5

    Played the first online RoboCup after the event in Bordeaux couldn’t take place, and finished fourth in the lower tournament.

  7. 2019–20Crew 4

    Improved the robots’ mechanics and made the software and electronics easier to maintain and more durable for later crews. Its RoboCup in Bordeaux was postponed.

  8. 2018–19Crew 3

    Rebuilt the robots’ software and communication, the base every later crew worked from. Fifth overall at RoboCup in Sydney, the team’s best result at the time.

  9. 2017–18Crew 2

    Improved the robots a lot. Seventh overall at RoboCup and third for most innovative team.

  10. 2016–17Crew 1

    Started the team and finished 14th at its first RoboCup.

Named after a place on Mars

Cydonia is a region of Mars at 40.7° N, 9.5° W. Viking 1 photographed it in 1976, and one hill in that picture looked like a face.

In 2007 the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter looked again at thirty times the detail. It was an ordinary mesa. We liked that story: look closer, and measure.
